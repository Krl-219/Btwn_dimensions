#### Note 
On Thursday and Friday of last week, I was in Montreal for [_Attention: Govern or Be Governed_](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMDYmZD1tMXQzYjFp.HG6IRXhPXqxhvpiPDlFwnQlDDNprPKxDHYZWrcP24mM), a conference hosted by McGill University’s Center for Media, Technology and Democracy. The event brought together lawmakers, civil society leaders, academics, journalists, and technologists—including many from the Tech Policy Press contributor community—to discuss the relationship between technology and the crisis of democracy.

One of the many speakers in an ambitious program organized by **Taylor Owen** and his team was Nobel Peace Prize laureate and journalist **Maria Ressa**. Ressa, like many of the speakers, emphasized how precarious democracy is around the world at the moment, and the difficult position countries like Canada are in as they respond to the threat from the United States.

“I think we're right at the precipice where fascism can win globally,” said Ressa, “and when that happens, if it happens, that will be for a decade or more, because it's hard to fight back. So, the question that you have is, again, short-term gain or long-term sacrifice, which one? As a leader in your country, prepare.”

But, she said, there is one proven way to generate hope.

“Optimism and hope come from action,” she said. “So, if you're feeling hemmed in, and you're worried about the future, act.”

Multiple contributors this week considered the relationship between the US tech power and state power, and how to defend democracies—from Brazil to Canada to Europe and beyond—from this increasingly dangerous combination:

- Since the US election in November 2024, the technology industry has distinguished itself in the race to capitulate to and enable the Trump administration, writes **Paul Barrett**. No gesture of obsequiousness or loyalty has been too great or trivial for the moguls of Silicon Valley, he says. [But there are steps we can each take to fight back](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMDcmZD11MWMwdTFm.RhVklZXTxxPoBJUMxjeHPD4mUy226xx1_ImY9puTpmY). “These are not normal times,” says Barrett. “We cannot do business as usual. The tech industry is selling out to a dangerous political force. Those who favor democracy need to recognize what is happening and respond as best we can.”
    

- Canada is waking up to the risks of relying on US tech giants for its digital infrastructure, writes Canadian SHIELD Institute researcher and Canadian Anti-Monopoly Project fellow **Emily Osborne**. As Canada pursues a sovereign cloud, clarity and control —not marketing—[should guide the path to digital independence](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMDgmZD1vN3UxejFj.3hBvkAcrVFsRk3buvAN9llPVVqtqXJGlPeYKgxh0680), she writes.
    

- The cozy alliance between Washington and Silicon Valley is creating an unprecedented concentration of power. In this new landscape, [Europe’s bid for digital sovereignty is a bid for democracy itself](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMDkmZD16MGE1bTN4.Z2tqJ9a_7miKRCRK6Dfnmo2gM8adsJJcK6ZKqsSwdkE), writes Open Market Institute's **Courtney C. Radsch** in one of multiple new pieces in our "Ideas for Europe’s Future" series. (Read more about the series [here.](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMTAmZD1oN3ExbjBh.rkRVzIN0fXSr7u-Bi5Rms53Mp9hJr395EruBpGuHiLE)) “Over the past year, the US government and dominant tech firms launched a campaign to undermine digital legislation in Europe, equating regulation with censorship to protect American dominance while undermining the democratic sovereignty of its allies,” writes Radsch, who is on the board of Tech Policy Press.
    

- Europe should stop signing trade deals that restrict its future policy options. It must fight the portrayal of tech regulations as trade barriers and instead [highlight how its laws strengthen digital trade](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMTEmZD1zOXk0YjBq.Xpf9Zb3VC0nCUxJKs3SkMf7xCt51a5feJp0xsi42OCg), writes Open Market Institute's **Audrey Stienon**.
    

- To begin reclaiming control of its future, Europe must [chart a path toward digital sovereignty](https://click.mailerlite.com/link/c/YT0yODY0MTAyNDI0NDYzODA4MDY1JmM9djhxNSZiPTE0ODg5ODQzMTImZD15NXczdTl0.CfqzEZnvHGQ5AAKW8OnSZ4q3aYj-9paIuzVR4qNj7ks). **Johnny Ryan**, Director of Enforce at the Irish Council for Civil Liberties, argues there are three key levers to achieve this: politics, policing, and procurement.
